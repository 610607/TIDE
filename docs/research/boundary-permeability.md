# Emergent Boundaries and Channel-Specific Permeability

Second-pass stream A; searched and read on **2026-11-15**. Scope: design input for TIDE, not a novelty or prior-art assessment. Six primary works are included. No simulations or kernel/prototype changes were made. The PDF skill was used for extraction and page-grounded inspection; original PDF bytes were retained.

## Result and scope

The strongest design lesson is to distinguish **a boundary's spatial organization**, **the interaction laws that make it selective**, and **evolution of those laws or of their local realization**. These are different achievements. The papers below offer examples of self-produced enclosures, material-dependent exclusion, graded leakage, and patterns whose extent is not stored as an individual object. They do not jointly demonstrate an evolved barrier independently controlling VM write, damage, data-read, instruction-fetch, material, and signal channels.

For the user's candidate, a useful question is therefore: *Can locally constructed and maintained structure alter channel-specific interactions enough to sustain a body or group, while retaining necessary intake, perception, and reproduction?* This remains a TIDE proposal. It does not replace the current local address-based VM contract. In candidate option B, threads and site ownership remain supplied; the spatial extent and interaction properties of a body or group can vary. That is a weaker claim than emergence of every level of individuality.

Four qualifications matter immediately:

1. **Emergent enclosure is compatible with strongly designed ingredients.** Catalysts, selective mobility, bonding geometry, decay, and resource flux are imposed in these models. An outcome can self-organize without its enabling physics having evolved.
2. **Maintenance need not be an explicit upkeep tax.** Consuming building material, losing components to decay, and failing to repair before contents escape can impose a maintenance burden. These are distinct from charging CPU time or an energy token per boundary site.
3. **Selectivity is often supplied.** Varela/McMullin supply species permeability; Hutton supplies size/bond-dependent passage. Ono's density-dependent walls yield graded leakage, but mobility parameters are experimental settings. None of these is a demonstrated evolved six-channel permission system.
4. **Local sensing is not necessarily propagated sensing.** A finite convolution neighborhood directly reads a field over its support. It does not by itself model travel time, absorption, or occlusion by an intervening wall. This distinction is explicit in the Flow-Lenia and Egbert readings below.

### A1. Varela, Maturana, and Uribe (1974)

**Citation/version/status.** F. G. Varela, H. R. Maturana, and R. Uribe. “Autopoiesis: The organization of living systems, its characterization and a model.” *BioSystems* 5(4):187–196. Published journal article, DOI [10.1016/0303-2647(74)90031-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/0303-2647(74)90031-8). Read the original publisher scan hosted by the University of Sussex, not a later summary or retypeset copy: [local PDF](../references/pdf/varela-maturana-uribe-1974-autopoiesis.pdf), [institutional source PDF](https://users.sussex.ac.uk/~inmanh/adsys10/Readings/Varela_etal_-_1974_-_Autopoiesis_the_organization_of_the_living_characterization_n_model_-_Biosystems.pdf).

**Read.** PDF pp. 1–10: §§3–7, six-point diagnostic key, and full algorithm appendix. Rendered pp. 3–4 were inspected to check Schema I and the formation/repair figure against noisy OCR.

**Source findings.** The model supplies substrate S, catalyst K, and link L types. K catalyzes `2 S -> L`; L bonds into chains and decays back to substrate. A closed chain can retain K while permitting substrate passage. The enclosed production of new links can replace decayed boundary components, thereby sustaining the conditions for production. Figure 1 shows formation around a catalyst; Figure 2 shows repair after decay. The paper distinguishes maintenance of an organization from reproduction; these example runs are not a demonstrated autonomous cell-division cycle. (§§5–7; PDF pp. 3–5.)

**Designer ingredients and limits.** Motion hierarchy, restricted passage through bonded links, maximum bonding degree/angle, catalyst presence, and decay are supplied. The catalyst is a permanent constituent rather than a demonstrated self-produced genome. Link decay makes a longer boundary more exposed to failure while production is limited to one link per catalyst per step; this couples size and repair demand without an energy currency. The appendix sequences motion, production, decay, and bonding; it is not a modern synchronous snapshot/conflict contract. Its description also contains an omission subsequently diagnosed by A2. A picture of a closed chain is insufficient evidence that the printed qualitative chemistry reproduces the reported repair behavior.

### A2. McMullin and Varela (1997)

**Citation/version/status.** Barry McMullin and Francisco J. Varela. “Rediscovering Computational Autopoiesis.” In P. Husbands and I. Harvey, eds., *Fourth European Conference on Artificial Life*, pp. 38–47. Published conference work; the inspected final author-hosted revised paper is dated April 1997 and identifies the ECAL-97 presentation. The current 10-page PDF was recompiled in 2011; that file date does not make it a 2011 study. [Local PDF](../references/pdf/mcmullin-varela-1997-rediscovering-autopoiesis.pdf), [author/institution PDF](https://www.eeng.dcu.ie/~alife/bmcm-ecal97/bmcm-ecal97.pdf), [institutional publication record](https://www.santafe.edu/research/results/papers/1436-rediscovering-computational-autopoiesis).

**Read.** PDF pp. 1–10, especially §§2–8, including all ten run accounts and the §7 footnote restricting the inhibition rule.

**Source findings.** In their independent SCL implementation, the uncorrected qualitative chemistry repeatedly produces internal chains that become immobile, clog the compartment, and leave no mobile repair stock. They recover an interaction present in an early original program but absent from the published descriptions: inhibit bonding **to a free L** adjacent to a doubly bonded L. Free links alongside an intact chain remain available; links at an end or rupture can bond. Applying inhibition to singly bonded links as well would prevent repair. This is a local state-dependent reaction condition, not a globally defined protected inside. (§§6–7; PDF pp. 3–6.)

**Limits and failures.** These experiments start with a supplied 12-link enclosure and one catalyst in a 15×15 torus; spontaneous formation is explicitly deferred. The added rule permits extended maintenance in some runs, but not indefinite survival: early failure, inward spirals, catalyst escape, and a geometrically closed cavity with no production space all occur. The five corrected runs must not be represented as five durable successes. Fixed initial PRNG states are distributed, but cross-backend bitwise replay is not established. This report is an independent replication/discrepancy study of the model, not a replication or code test performed for TIDE. (§§3,5,8; PDF pp. 2–3,6–9.)

### A3. Ono and Ikegami (2000)

**Citation/version/status.** Naoaki Ono and Takashi Ikegami. “Self-maintenance and Self-reproduction in an Abstract Cell Model.” *Journal of Theoretical Biology* 206(2):243–253. Published article, DOI [10.1006/jtbi.2000.2121](https://doi.org/10.1006/jtbi.2000.2121). [Local PDF](../references/pdf/ono-ikegami-2000-self-maintenance-reproduction.pdf). The exact publisher article is publicly linked as `ono_jtheorbiol_2000.pdf` by the [author's institutional publications page](https://www.sacral.c.u-tokyo.ac.jp/publications): [public file](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1x9BT7tV4Q8gRhu924FkssJ1nsOjmd9Ka/view), [ordinary download](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1x9BT7tV4Q8gRhu924FkssJ1nsOjmd9Ka).

**Read.** Full PDF pp. 1–11: §§2–4, Figs. 1–10, Appendix A transition probabilities and update algorithm; Appendix B checked for the non-spatial comparison. **This paper's implemented reaction space is one-dimensional.** The 1999 two-dimensional ECAL work is a separate publication, screened below, not another version of this article.

**Source findings.** Five particle types realize an autocatalytic A/E cycle, membrane M production from resource R, common decay to waste W, and W-to-R replenishment. Hydrophobic M and hydrophilic A/E/R repel through rates depending on local and neighboring particle counts. M clusters become walls; they retain catalysts sufficiently for continuing membrane production. No cell-object extent or division instruction is needed. Thin membranes can leak A, which initiates new compartments outside (“open” reproduction). Changing the supplied mobility ratio so A/E move less readily than M/R can instead cause growth and a new internal wall, repeatedly splitting a compartment (“closed” reproduction). (§§2–3; PDF pp. 2–8.)

**Selectivity, cost, and limits.** Permeability is graded through wall density and mobility, rather than an all-or-none individual ownership gate. Membrane-production parameters and species mobilities are imposed controls, not shown evolving. A/E escape, insufficient M production, and decay can jointly erase the compartment; a stable compartment is not automatically a reproducing one. The model has 100 sites, always 100 particles per site: movement swaps a selected counterparticle back, and reactions change particle state. Material count is explicit, whereas W-to-R replenishment represents external resource/energy supply rather than a conserved energy budget. Appendix A separates chemical transition evaluation from diffusion, but movement visits sites in random order; it does not establish TIDE-style synchronous conflict semantics. (§§3–4, Appendix A; PDF pp. 5–10.)

### A4. Hutton (2007)

**Citation/version/status.** Tim J. Hutton. “Evolvable Self-Reproducing Cells in a Two-Dimensional Artificial Chemistry.” *Artificial Life* 13(1):11–30, DOI [10.1162/artl.2007.13.1.11](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl.2007.13.1.11). The inspected [local PDF](../references/pdf/hutton-2007-evolvable-self-reproducing-cells.pdf) is a **32-page author manuscript dated 10 January 2007**, recovered from [CiteSeerX](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=8b4989c68ddc2c7f3fd5936bc7cbf069b46b359b&repid=rep1&type=pdf); its pagination must not be reported as journal pagination. The [author's Squirm3 repository](https://github.com/timhutton/squirm3) independently identifies the same paper and one implementation. Its old linked author PDF returns 404.

**Read.** Selected full text: PDF pp. 1–10,13–21,25–27; §§2–3, Table 1, growth and enzyme rules in Figs. 11–15, “Physics,” experiment setup, and discussion/conclusions. Repository README checked for provenance only; implementation source was not audited or run.

**Source findings.** A supplied ancestor contains a closed bonded loop and a gene string attached at two membrane points. Thirty-four designed rules copy the string, move attachments, contract the enclosure, and separate daughters. Rule R35 inserts/removes membrane atoms from/to the surrounding material. A further designed decoder produces enzyme pairs; pairing retains them inside while single unbonded resource atoms can enter. Thus exclusion depends on material configuration, not a cell-ID permission table. The paper explicitly says this chosen simple loop membrane lacks self-assembly and self-repair. (§§2–3; PDF pp. 4–10,13–17.)

**Implementation and limits.** The continuous-space realization disables overlap forces for unbonded atoms to allow passage; the lattice realization rejects moves that overstretch or cross bonds, allows reactions only across permitted local geometry, and updates atoms in random order. Competing applicable reactions are chosen asynchronously at random. Consequently “physical selectivity” is still made by designer rules, not derived from a single general collision law. Ordinary reactions rearrange a fixed population of atom types, but no explicit chemical energy conservation is established. Experiments use imposed floods that reset atoms and connected atoms to raw states and break bonds; those are external environmental forcing, not endogenous maintenance. The paper explicitly reports practical evolvability limitations and does not demonstrate evolution of permeability or a wholly discovered replication cycle. (§3 “Physics,” §4 setup, §5; PDF pp. 7,18–21,25–27.)

### A5. Egbert, Barandiaran, and Di Paolo (2010)

**Citation/version/status.** Matthew D. Egbert, Xabier E. Barandiaran, and Ezequiel A. Di Paolo. “A Minimal Model of Metabolism-Based Chemotaxis.” *PLOS Computational Biology* 6(12):e1001004, published 2 December 2010, DOI [10.1371/journal.pcbi.1001004](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1001004). [Local PDF](../references/pdf/egbert-barandiaran-dipaolo-2010-metabolism-chemotaxis.pdf), [publisher article](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1001004), [publisher PDF](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1001004&type=printable). Open-access publisher version, 17 pages.

**Read.** Selected full text: PDF pp. 1,4–8,13–14,16; Methods, Tables 1–2, movement and environment definitions, Experiment 1, selected later figures, and discussion. Not all six experimental result narratives were read in full.

**Source findings.** Each supplied bacterium is a position/orientation plus internal chemical concentrations. Local environmental resource concentration drives uptake at a fixed rate, independent of internal concentration. Metabolism produces C, which directly increases the chance of tumbling; poor metabolic conditions produce more running. This “selective stopping” can accumulate agents near metabolizable resources without reading a gradient vector or adding a dedicated receptor pathway. History and combinations of resources can matter through internal dynamics. A local stimulus need not have an externally assigned attractive/repellent label. (Methods, Experiment 1; PDF pp. 5–8.)

**Limits for boundary and locality claims.** The compartment is supplied; no membrane is assembled, maintained, divided, or evolved here. Uptake coefficient `Din=0.2` is fixed. Environmental fields are held constant and are not depleted by agents. Degradation removes species into untracked products; movement energy cost is explicitly left for more realistic future models. Euler integration uses timestep 0.01 and movement includes stochastic tumbling, so this is not an integer accounting or bitwise replay prescription. Local uptake is a causal contact with a prescribed field; the paper does not solve finite-speed propagation or show a protection/perception tradeoff caused by an evolved wall. (Methods, Table 2, final discussion; PDF pp. 6–7,16.)

### A6. Plantec et al. (2025), Flow-Lenia

**Citation/version/status.** Erwan Plantec, Gautier Hamon, Mayalen Etcheverry, Bert Wang-Chak Chan, Pierre-Yves Oudeyer, and Clément Moulin-Frier. “Flow-Lenia: Emergent evolutionary dynamics in mass conservative continuous cellular automata.” *Artificial Life* 31(2):228–248, DOI [10.1162/artl_a_00471](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00471). Read the cached accepted manuscript [arXiv:2506.08569v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08569v1), dated 10 June 2025: [local PDF](../references/pdf/plantec-et-al-2025-flow-lenia-evolution-v1.pdf), [original online PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.08569v1). No re-download.

**Read.** Selected full text: PDF pp. 1–12,23–24, especially §§2–3.1 (Eqs. 1–8), Table 1, §4.3 model variations, and final limitations. PDF pp. 6–12 correspond to printed manuscript pp. 4–10. This pass did not re-audit all evolutionary activity results or official code.

**Source findings.** Activations represent concentrations. Convolution and growth functions define an affinity map; its gradient draws matter, while a density-dependent negative concentration gradient prevents excessive crowding. Reintegration redistributes incoming mass by normalized overlap weights. Pattern extent is not stored as a container or cell ID. Local parameter vectors move with matter; when different parameter sets reach a cell, the default rule samples a source parameter according to incoming amounts (a deterministic argmax variant is also described). This is a mechanism for conversion and competition, not immunity attached to an individual. (§§2–3.1; PDF pp. 6–9.)

**Limits and sensing distinction.** The finite neighborhood radius R constrains convolution dependencies. It does **not** mean a material or signal must physically travel from every sampled location to the sensing location. Food is explicitly sensed by additional kernels; a wall that blocks transport is not thereby specified to block this convolutional information. Reintegration separately transports matter and is computed over a Chebyshev-distance-<5 incoming neighborhood for efficiency. A portable implementation would need to establish the displacement bound/normalization under its actual parameters rather than infer exact conservation from a truncated sum. The vanilla equations conserve continuous mass algebraically; they do not establish integer conservation or bitwise CPU/GPU equivalence. Default stochastic parameter mixing further requires event/RNG semantics. The food variant adds decay, conversion, and externally inserted food; the dissipative variant adds/removes patches. Neither should be described as the closed vanilla mass budget. (§§3–4.3; PDF pp. 7–12.)

**Boundary interpretation.** Cohesive concentrations and parameter domains provide a useful alternative to a predeclared body extent, but the article does not supply a distinct membrane with independently tunable permeability. Parameters are not individuals: the discussion notes persistent patterns composed of several parameter sets and leaves improved individual definitions open. No protection-versus-perception result is established merely by observing a localized pattern. (Discussion; PDF pp. 23–24.)

## What “permeability” covers in this corpus

| Candidate TIDE channel | Direct evidence in the six works | Unsupported inference to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Material transport | A1/A2 species passage; A3 concentration/mobility-dependent leakage; A4 single-atom versus bonded-molecule passage; A6 advective mass redistribution | A single protective bit summarizes all passage, or graded leakage has already evolved in these experiments |
| Damage / destructive state change | Local decay, bond rewriting, catalyst-mediated reactions, and externally forced resets are distinct mechanisms | Blocking an invading particle automatically blocks every damaging reaction or externally imposed event |
| Write | A4 enzymes can affect nearby reactants subject to physics; none is a VM allocation-write contract | A material envelope establishes memory ownership or write authorization |
| Data read | A4 reads a neighboring sequence through designed reaction steps; A6 convolves channel fields | Retaining a genome or blocking material also prevents external information sampling |
| Instruction fetch / execution borrowing | No direct VM instruction-fetch analogue is established in these six works | A chemical membrane justifies a particular neighbor-code execution permission |
| Signal / perception | A5 integrates local uptake through metabolism; A6 directly evaluates neighboring field/food values | Finite kernel support proves finite-speed signal travel, path attenuation, or occlusion by a boundary |

The relevant distinction for A3 is between **dependency range**, **transport distance per step**, and **obstruction along a path**. A bounded stencil can impose a finite influence cone across repeated synchronous steps; it still reads all sites in its stencil at that step without material traveling through intermediate sites. A path-mediated material/signal process can instead be attenuated or blocked on each traversed edge. These are analytical distinctions drawn from the equations, not evidence that either design is preferable for TIDE.

## TIDE candidates, explicitly not agreed requirements

**C1: Local, maintained interaction modifiers.** Represent a candidate boundary by ordinary local state and local transitions. A construction step transfers a named amount from a local material/energy source into a barrier component; decay transfers it to a specified sink/residue. Test no-decay, decay-without-repair, and decay-with-repair separately. A1/A2 warn that a successful construction picture does not establish repair: immobilized repair stock and inhibited endpoints are explicit negative cases.

**C2: A vector of channel effects.** A boundary can change write, damage, data-read, instruction-fetch, material, and signal interactions independently. Do not inherit `body_id`, group membership, or analyst labels into these physical rules. For a graded mechanism, define whether strength changes success probability, flux capacity, resistance, latency, or a reaction rate: those are different transitions. Source support is strongest for material permeability; applying this structure to VM permissions is a project proposal.

**C3: Test supplied selectivity separately from evolved realization.** First ask whether fixed local laws allow a known program to construct/maintain useful spatial structure. Later ask whether mutation changes the amount, layout, production policy, or composition of that structure. Evolving composition under fixed pairwise laws is not the same as evolving the laws themselves. A3 motivates leakage and retention as measurable outcomes without requiring a hard inside/outside predicate.

**C4: Separate protection and perception controls.** A future comparison could pair a transport-blocking barrier with (a) direct neighborhood reads, (b) an explicitly propagated/attenuated signal field, or (c) uptake-mediated metabolic state. Measure attack success, resource influx, response latency, and persistence. A protective wall may reduce intake or perception only when the defined channel dynamics cause that coupling. None of the six works proves a universal protection/perception tradeoff.

**C5: Option B retains a supplied execution substrate.** Thread identity and site ownership can remain given while connected components, cooperative extent, barrier layout, and persistence vary. Diagnose bodies/groups from trajectories and intervention-defined dependencies in analysis. This does not claim emergent CPU ownership, spontaneous replicator origin, or complete autopoietic reproduction. A supplied ancestor remains compatible with the project's present experimental scope.

**C6: Minimal future expressivity checks.** With mutation disabled, consider a supplied builder, an inert barrier, an attacker, and a resource source. Check formation, decay, repair, a breach, and reproduction separately. Possible falsifying outcomes include no advantage over an equal-cost allocation, repair stock trapping itself, a boundary that starves its builder, direct reads bypassing an intended sensory cost, and splitting that destroys the necessary execution state. These are proposed checks only; no such tests were run in this literature pass.

## Open R0 questions for discussion

- What local state changes a channel, and at which point in an operation is it sampled? Does construction in the same synchronous step affect another program's attempted action?
- What is the conserved budget, its unit, and each source/sink? Does a failed build reserve or consume anything? Where do decay products go when local storage is full?
- What happens when several builders, movers, or attackers target one edge/site? Can exchange cycles proceed, and what deterministic order resolves incompatible intents?
- Which channels require adjacency, a direct neighborhood lookup, a clear path, or stepwise propagation? Is there distance attenuation, and do material barriers affect it?
- How is graded strength interpreted without floating-point platform drift? How are RNG draws identified, probability saturation handled, and equal-strength conflicts resolved?
- Does maintenance consume resources continuously, only on repair, or only during construction? The alternatives should be named variables, not merged into one cost.
- How do damage, residual unowned bytes, and reclamation affect local boundary state? Does destruction release material, expose code, or both?
- If body/group extent is analytical, what event activates a newborn thread and what distinguishes fission of a group from mere spatial fragmentation?
