# Local accounting and the formation of groups: second design pass

Search and reading date: 2026-11-15. Status: **design input; no novelty assessment and no rule acceptance**. This stream includes exactly five primary works. It uses the PDF skill to inspect cached original texts and selected complete rendered pages, plus a pinned, read-only inspection of official DISHTINY source. No simulation, kernel/prototype modification, or Git remote operation was performed.

The question is narrower than whether a model exhibits cooperation: **which primitive quantities and transitions let a persistent, spatially extended, cooperating entity form, and which parts of that entity were already supplied by the designer?** For TIDE's candidate option B, execution threads and site ownership are given; body extent, shape, territorial division, and coordination among threads are intended to remain variable. Nothing below changes the agreed local VM or settles R0.

## Findings that matter for this discussion

1. **A cap, a throughput budget, and a conserved stock are different mechanisms.** Coreworld replenishes local execution credit; Avida allocates a finite instruction throughput and optionally supplies depletable reaction pools; the 2010 Stringmol specification supplies global energy and assumes unlimited building symbols; spatial Stringmol in 2021 removes that energy gate. DISHTINY supplies and dissipates resource while charging births. None of these five establishes a globally closed, locally transported material economy for TIDE.
2. **Functional collectives can arise without a group object.** Coreworld's recurrent instruction/pointer structures and Stringmol's mutual replicators demonstrate this limited point. They do not demonstrate an evolvable membrane, territorial ownership, or a new higher-level unit with a specified collective birth/death transition.
3. **DISHTINY is the closest included precedent for evolving spatial group life histories, but it deliberately scaffolds groups.** Inherited IDs, recognition instructions, reward waves that follow same-ID neighbors, one or two grouping levels, and group aging are supplied. Shape, reproductive restraint, resource-sharing policies, and several propagule strategies evolve within that scaffold. The four directional controllers per cell are also supplied, rather than an evolved count of threads.
4. **Selective exchange has several meanings.** Stringmol selects binding and execution paths; DISHTINY filters messages and throttles resource transfers. Neither is evidence that a costly material barrier or its maintenance cost emerged. In DISHTINY, rejected resource remains at the sender; message-filter tags expire, but an energetic permeability-maintenance charge is not established by the inspected methods.

### Controls that were actually reported

**Coreworld:** §§4.1-4.3 identify high regeneration, large operational radius and small resource radius as fertile settings, but do not provide a clean local-credit-on versus local-credit-off effect estimate. Defaults given are `(N,L,Δr,r_max,R_res)=(3584,220,0.5,0.5,3)` (§4.1, PDF p. 125). Venus I and II are examined, with examples of persistent MOV/SPL and jump-dominated structures (§4.3). These examples are not a matched scheduler ablation. In Luna (§5, PDF p. 131), a much simpler instruction set and one-execution-per-address cap produce takeover by a self-copying instruction at small radius; other cooperative structures are overwritten. Increasing radius changes that outcome. Local resource constraints or finite pointer lifetime are proposed ways to reduce self-sufficiency; the chapter passage does **not** report a finite-lifetime rescue experiment. Biased initial conditions and continuing random pointer injection also contribute to activity; seeded replicators typically malfunction after roughly 200 iterations (§4.2), so long-lived instruction structures should not be described as preserved seeded organisms.

**Avida:** §3.3 states that depletable pools permit diverse subpopulations to coexist, citing prior work; this platform article is not itself a factorial experiment establishing how depletion changes spatial morphology. §3.2 reports that local neighborhoods limit growth and permit somewhat greater diversity than well-stirred placement, and that replacement policy affects evolutionary speed. The optional instruction-count death rule prevents a specific artifact: without independent death, organisms that lose replication can remain indefinitely if births cease. That persistence is not self-maintenance. The integrated scheduler can also synchronize populations at low mutation rates (§3.1); the probabilistic scheduler is an available comparison, not proof of equivalence.

**Stringmol v0.2:** Appendix C (printed pp. 38-39, PDF pp. 39-40) explicitly compares inverse-length versus constant decay rates and unbound-only versus all-state decay. Inverse-length plus unbound-only favors long, indefinitely bound molecules; inverse-length plus all-state yields many long species with little functional difference; constant-rate plus unbound-only can end in nonterminating bound pairs with no further evolution; constant-rate plus all-state yields turnover of dominant species. These are decay-protocol observations, not evidence that closed material accounting promotes complexity. §11.2 describes 1,000 mutation-on trials that terminate at extinction and gives extinction-time distributions, alongside parasites, mutual replication and other trajectories. An energy-on/off ablation is not supplied by these passages.

**Spatial Stringmol:** §2.2 reports **20 runs: 12 extinct and 8 still executing at two million timesteps**. The four treatments vary rectangular arena dimensions and initial clustered versus dispersed pairs, five replicates per combination (§5.2). Spatial wavefronts and changing interaction strategies are observed; there is no claim here that every spatial replicate survives. §2.9 reports recurring slowdowns, self-scanning, partner excursions and some mutually dependent replicators. The well-mixed growth assays in Figure 5 disable mutation to characterize sampled genotypes; they are not a matched evolution run isolating space while retaining all other 2010 settings. In particular, the energy change prevents treating “2010 versus 2021” as a one-mechanism experiment.

**DISHTINY:** the four treatments combine one/two hereditary levels with wave-mediated/uniform per-cell inflow. Wave rewards are removed and uniform inflow adjusted to about `+0.0175` in the even treatments; nested-wave uses baseline `+0.0051` and `+0.2` successful wave rewards at each level; flat-wave uses `+0.6` rewards (author supplement §§6.2, 6.8). Forty evolutionary replicates per treatment were run; all cross-replicate comparisons use update 262,144 because four runs stopped there (§2.4). All four treatments show reduced interior reproduction and higher mean sharing to registered kin, including non-direct kin (§3; supplement §§6.11-6.12). This is evidence that the particular wave reward is not necessary for these measured cooperative traits **under retained kin recognition, aging and spatial competition**, not evidence that kin recognition is unnecessary.

DISHTINY's case studies supply more targeted evidence: one cell-cell-messaging case and its resource-sharing knockout lose to wild type in 20/20 competitions each (§3.3); a resource-gradient-sensing knockout also loses 20/20 (§3.4). The Nested-Even string-shaped morphology is disrupted by intracellular-message knockout, but wild type wins only 6/20 competitions, so a fitness benefit was not demonstrated (§3.5). Apoptosis knockouts and spatially/temporally uniform apoptosis controls support the adaptiveness of targeted apoptosis in two selected strains (§3.6). These selected case studies do not estimate the frequency of all possible life histories, and do not test a costly physical permeability barrier.

### Exact failure and boundary details to retain

- **Coreworld:** inadequate execution credit deletes the pointer rather than leaving a suspended, living organism. Off-radius effects wrap into the permitted operational range, rather than failing. The finite queue's overflow semantics and exact shared-credit debit are not specified in the inspected chapter. The claim that conflict details “do not affect” dynamics is not a substitute for replay semantics or a demonstrated policy sweep.
- **Avida:** failed commands are essentially ignored and counted; configurable punishment is possible. `h-divide` fails if parent/child would have fewer than 10 instructions, allocation has not occurred, fewer than half the parent instructions were executed, fewer than half the child's memory positions were copied, or configured size limits fail (§2.2.3). Successful division discards unused trailing allocation and resets the parent CPU by default. Instruction-level failure can still consume cycles (§4.2). A task reaction below its minimum available resource stops (§4.4). None of these failures is an allocation conflict over public terrain.
- **Stringmol v0.2:** unsuccessful binding leaves unbound molecules while costing one energy unit per attempt; energy shortage gates execution. Copy exceeding maximum pointer/string limit `Ω` ends the reaction (§10.6). `cleave` makes a product and adjusts downstream pointers (§10.7); that section says no action when the flow pointer is at either endpoint. However, §4's state-chart prose mentions destruction by “cleave-zero.” This internal specification inconsistency should remain visible; no code inspection was made to resolve it. Also §4's closing sentence refers to length-governed decay despite the earlier constant-rate v0.2 rule and Appendix C. Use the explicit versioned rule and flag the inconsistency.
- **Spatial Stringmol:** no local product vacancy means the product is discarded. The paper says a new product may bind only next timestep. It does not fully define conflicting simultaneous product placements or every same-timestep binding/decay tie. These details require its reported 0.2.3.4 implementation; they must not be imported from the 2010 specification without checking.
- **DISHTINY paper/supplement:** insufficient available resource makes a birth attempt do nothing. Expired group membership does not silently kill the cell or erase all reproductive ability: reproduction escalates to a higher ID-refresh level, and even fully expired cells can found new groups. Somatic daughters increment inherited group generation counters; new IDs reset their counters. Time also increments counters, enforcing eventual loss of somatic regeneration and wave harvesting. Baseline inflow remains. Apoptosis kills the cell at an update boundary; neighboring heirs may receive residual stock and a recovery credit. The death of a bridging cell can geometrically fragment an ID group without giving its pieces new IDs.

A visually enclosed patch, a same-ID hereditary group, a binary reaction, a computationally interdependent network, and a territory are therefore separate observables. Treating all of them as “a body” would conceal which transition actually exists.

## Candidate design inputs for TIDE; all remain proposals

These mechanisms are separable. They do not imply importing a DISHTINY group ID, an Avida merit reward, a Stringmol reaction kernel, or a CPM vesicle into the agreed local VM.

| Candidate | Minimal explicit transition to discuss | Failure / accounting questions before implementation | Mutation-free discriminator, proposed only |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Local execution credit | An external source supplies integer credit to sites; a thread may request a priced action from a defined local credit neighborhood. Debit and accepted action commit together. | Which sites pay, in what order, and who wins overlapping withdrawals? Does failure wait, no-op, or kill? What happens at caps and on death? Count supplied, buffered, stored, spent and discarded credit separately. | Two fixed programs with equal instruction demands but different spatial concentration; compare local credit with a matched global throughput budget. Persistence of activity alone is insufficient. |
| Locally transported substrate | A copy/extension consumes explicitly typed units from an allowed nearby source and records them in the constructed state; release moves units to a named local residual pool. | Is substrate closed or externally replenished? Can a dead owned site retain code while releasing substrate? Which quantity is embodied by a byte, allocation or thread? Transport, decay and overflow need exact sources/sinks. | Copying plus reverse release should close an integer ledger under collisions and deaths. This is a proposed accounting check, not an experiment already run. |
| Costly selective permeability | A site/interface admits a specified action class using a local, state-dependent rule. Setting/maintaining/using that selectivity has an explicit charge, if cost is the intended variable. | Message admission, reading, fetching, writing, material transport and territorial entry are different channels. What can sense a partner without paying? Is denied traffic refunded, retained at source or dissipated? Does an interface travel with ownership or remain in space? | Hold recognition fixed; compare zero versus positive selectivity costs and one channel at a time. Use cooperative and exploitative fixed programs before mutation. Neither Stringmol recognition nor DISHTINY's tag filters supplies this material-cost result. |
| A spatially extended cooperative entity | Ownership remains at the given primitive level; analysis observes coordinated thread sets, flows and persistence without giving the set a behavior-changing ecological label. | Define thread creation, ownership release, site capture and instruction-fetch rights locally. Avoid making membership, body size, a centroid or group fitness a hidden global operation. Is territorial division a local growth process, simultaneous relabeling, or something else? | Compare known independent and reciprocally dependent thread arrangements; remove one local dependency and test reproductive recovery. A shared lineage ID or visual adjacency alone should not establish functional integration. |
| Propagule versus fragmentation | Founding a new local execution/ownership region is a transition; reproduction of an inferred larger entity is an analytical claim supported by subsequent development. | What exactly is inherited, split or left behind? Which executing threads belong to a daughter, and when do they activate? What happens if the parent region is disconnected before or after capture? | Fixed programs should express at least one viable local founding strategy under each tested rule. Track extinction, takeover and sterile fragments together with survivors. |

For an integer ledger, a proposed diagnostic identity is `stored + in_transit + embodied + cumulative_spent + cumulative_discarded = initial + cumulative_external_supply`, with terms included only for genuinely modeled quantities and no double counting. If apoptosis can recover a construction cost, that cost must have entered an embodied reserve earlier or the recovery must be declared a source. This is an accounting proposal derived from the comparison, not a conservation law established by the reviewed platforms.

The next R0 discussion still needs to settle snapshot visibility, overlapping allocation/resource claims, failure effects, newborn activation, neighborhood geometry, residual-code reclamation, ownership transitions, mutation operators and replay event identities. The present evidence supports testing local propagation and exchange policies; it does not select their parameters, prove novelty, or establish that emergent graded boundaries will occur.
