# Inherited neighbor dependence in TIDE

TIDE starts with an independently reproducing digital ancestor. In this local virtual machine, descendants evolved the ability to reproduce using a neighbor's instructions or data. Paired permission tests found lineages in which losing the relevant access eliminated reproduction. This answers the first existence question in the tested fields. Persistence across changes of program and the identity of the inherited material require separate measurements. [Research question](neighbor-dependence-rq.md) · [Kernel transitions](../r0-kernel-contract.md)

## What was inherited?

**Borrowed instructions.** In a prospective cohort, seven of 32 worlds produced a mother and daughter whose programs required neighbor instruction fetch in the standardized serial assay. The independent ancestor reproduced with access removed. In the positive assays, an exact repeating state without births resolved cases where a short timeout had been inconclusive. The eight confirmed same-program field families subsequently lost their last clonal member by tick 713. The seven surrounding fields were still reproducing at tick 4096. In four worlds the same bytes appeared again outside the tracked family. [Permission and cycle evidence](../../kernel/runs/horizon-01/README.md) · [Family extinction and recurrence](../../kernel/runs/persistence-01/README.md)

**Borrowed data.** In two independent five-byte fields, six consecutive individuals in a pedigree required external reads in their natural birth environments. One pedigree included five consecutive exact transfers from living readers. All nine tested chain reproducers could still use residual bytes when reading from occupied neighbors alone was denied. In six-byte fields, 22 of 1,024 pilot worlds and eight of 1,024 replication worlds produced a changed mother and daughter that both depended on external reading. In seed 19995 each generation used one living template. The changed daughter in that example inherited the behavior while receiving its bytes from another individual. Permission dependence refers to the focal in the tested environment. [Five-byte pedigrees](../../kernel/runs/inheritance-01/README.md) · [Six-byte natural replication](../../kernel/runs/six-origin-01/README.md)

**Who supplied the sequence?** In 1,490 of 2,048 stationary six-byte worlds, an individual made an intact copy of a genetically different living neighbor. Among 3,161 such births, 845 drew their template from an individual that produced zero direct offspring during its fully observed lifetime. The executor genealogy records who performed division. The material genealogy records whose bytes reached the offspring. A statement about inheritance needs to specify which link it follows. ## What the spatial experiments resolved

Supplying a distant active reader with an adjacent position yielded 50 exact births from 45 of 3,439 matched templates. The equal-distance, nonadjacent control yielded zero. Continuing movement had a second effect. Concentrating a matched set of exchange offers into bursts raised complete six-byte reading from 43.6% to 61.6% of qualifying starts in new worlds. Under the full-lifetime criterion, each schedule had one new world with a different-reader handoff. Contact and its duration affect individual copies. The spatial results leave the long-term inheritance of a changed behavior open. ## Boundary of the first result

The evidence establishes naturally arising, heritable dependence under specified local rules. Identical bytes can reappear after a family dies, and persistent sequences can be carried by individuals that never reproduce on their own. Increased copying and persistence of a behavior across a change in sequence are distinct outcomes. The experiments use supplied ancestors, bounded space and time, and substitution-only mutation. Long-term accumulation of adaptive innovations and open-ended evolution remain open questions.

The subsequent long-run study held the rules fixed and followed 1,024 new worlds for 8,192 ticks each. In four worlds, an uninterrupted read-dependent pedigree crossed a change of sequence and produced a confirmed descendant after the first carrier died. Every such later birth had returned to the original program. The longest unchanged path contained eight carriers. Thus the first-stage existence result extends to behavior surviving a transient sequence change. Retention of a new program across that same boundary remains open. [Prospective protocol](../../kernel/runs/trait-lineage-01/protocol.md) · [Long-run results and pedigrees](../../kernel/runs/trait-lineage-01/README.md)

A candidate structural comparison is whether a changed carrier and a compatible living template can be transmitted together to a daughter neighborhood. A paired treatment could preserve that association or distribute the same individuals separately, holding the number and sequences of transferred individuals fixed. The informative outcome would be a confirmed descendant born after the original carrier's death that still carries the changed program. This would test the consequences of supplying the association. Its spontaneous origin would remain a separate question.
