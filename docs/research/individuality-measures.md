# Inferring individuality and graded boundaries

Search and reading date: 2026-11-15. This bounded second pass supplies design input, not a novelty claim. Four primary works are included; alternate versions of the same work do not add a work. No simulations, kernel/prototype changes, or new kernel rules are reported.

## Result and scope

The strongest usable distinction is between **candidate generation**, **measurement**, and **causal validation**. None of the four works supplies an observer-free algorithm that discovers arbitrary moving bodies directly from VM bytes. Information-theoretic individuality gives continuous scores for a proposed system/environment split. AlChemy gives constructive, grammatical, and kinetic criteria for non-spatial organizations. Chemical organization theory (COT) tests closure and feasible maintenance of a specified species/reaction network. BFF tests a supplied fixed-length program through finite copy chains. These answer different questions and should remain separate measurements.

For the user's candidate direction, execution threads and site ownership may remain given primitives while group/body extent is inferred. Write, damage, read, fetch, material, and signal boundaries may differ; their constructibility, permeability, and physical costs remain proposals for discussion. This report does not turn that direction into an approved R0 contract. In particular, a statistical size penalty used to select a candidate boundary is not an implemented resource cost for constructing that boundary.

## C1: Operational candidates and hidden observer choices

### ITI: degrees of temporal integrity, conditional on a proposed split

**Source findings.** ITI treats individuality as information propagated from a system's past into its future, allowing continuous, nested, spatially distributed candidates. A finite system/environment Markov model supplies states `S_t`, `E_t`, and `S_(t+1)`. Its basic lenses are:

- Organismal individuality: `A* = I(S_(t+1); S_t)`.
- Colonial individuality: `A = I(S_(t+1); S_t | E_t)`.
- Environmental determination/non-closure: `nC = I(S_(t+1); E_t | S_t)`.

These are the source definitions, not ecological labels to place in kernel state. The partial-information decomposition interprets shared, unique, and synergistic contributions, but the authors explicitly do not select a unique decomposition convention; the three MI/conditional-MI expressions above remain directly defined without solving that dispute. Informational closure does not mean absence of causal coupling: environmental influences can be predictable from the system. See “The informational individual,” “Fine-grained decomposition,” and “Forms of individuality,” pp. 214–216 / PDF pp. 6–8.

The paper's boundary-expansion analysis is decisive: both `A*` and `A` can only increase or remain unchanged when variables are added to the system. Raw maximization therefore cannot identify a precise stopping boundary. The authors call for an additional size cost/regularizer and leave an optimal induction algorithm to future work. They also identify variable choice, coordinate frame, time scale, and robustness as unresolved practical requirements. See pp. 218–221 / PDF pp. 10–13, especially p. 219 and “Algorithmic implementation.”

**Observer choices and limitations.** An analyst still selects elementary variables, candidate subsets, environmental conditioning variables, temporal lag, sampling window, coarse-graining, estimator, and size penalty. An emergent group may be inferred among primitive sites/threads without a pre-given group label, but that is not inference without a partition search space. Co-moving versus fixed spatial coordinates can give different results for a moving body. Persistent inactive bytes may carry temporal information; a perfectly constant state has zero entropy and therefore zero MI. Neither high persistence nor a low `nC` proves adaptation, replication, a membrane, or causal independence. Common inputs and omitted state can create or conceal predictive relationships. These last examples are analytical failure cases, not new TIDE runs.

### AlChemy: non-spatial constructive organizations and a maintained periphery

**Source findings.** The working paper's objects are already individuated lambda expressions, identified through standardized normal forms under an explicit reduction procedure. Ordered pair application produces a new expression while retaining the reactants; accepted production is balanced by random dilution. Nontermination or exceeding reduction limits produces an elastic collision with no product. Functional/syntactic filters can also reject a product, and suppressing direct copying is an experimental condition used to obtain Level 1 organizations. Thus emergent organizations do not imply primitive objects or all selection conditions emerged without external specification. See §§5.1–5.3, working-paper pp. 12–16 / PDF pp. 17–21.

Section 5.4 defines set action `A o A`, a replacement map `m(A) = A o A`, and an accumulating map `M(A) = A union (A o A)`. Iteration of the latter generates the interaction closure. Section 6.2.2 defines self-maintenance by `A subseteq A o A` (Eq. 38), distinguishes generators from seeding sets, and describes a smallest self-maintaining center in the examples. Section 6.2.6 identifies grammar, algebraic regularities, and self-maintenance as the organizational criteria; their boundaries are explicitly non-spatial. See working-paper pp. 17–19, 22–28, 34–35 / PDF pp. 22–24, 27–33, 39–40.

This text supports a useful core/periphery distinction: a finite reactor realizes only part of the invariant subspace, with sparse peripheral species disappearing and being reconstructed. It also explicitly says constructive self-maintenance is necessary but insufficient for kinetic persistence. The observed unique center is an observation about their examples, not a theorem that every organization has one. A Level 2 claim involves supporting suborganizations and “glue”; the paper proposes inoculating an empty reactor with the putative centers to distinguish the organization from a transient conjunction. See working-paper pp. 26–28, 49–50 / PDF pp. 31–33, 54–55.

**Observer choices and limitations.** Species identity, normal-form equivalence, the collision rule, filtering, reduction budget, and the grammar/algebra used to describe an invariant subspace are inputs. Exhaustively listing successful reactions is not itself the compressed organizational description discussed in §6.2.1. Nor does observing a few reactions prove invariance for all possible members of an infinite grammar. The paper has no spatial diffusion, membrane, free-energy budget, or conserved mass in its minimal reactor (§5). Its non-spatial closure and graded kinetic occupancy are relevant analogies for body extent, not source evidence for channel-selective permeability or an implemented boundary cost.

### COT: species-set closure plus feasible maintenance flux

**Source findings in the 2005 text.** An algebraic chemistry is a specified species set and a multiset reaction relation. Inflow `empty -> a` and outflow `a -> empty` are reactions, so closure need not describe a physically sealed container. Definition 2 makes `C` closed if every rule whose reactant types are in `C` has all product types in `C`. Definitions 4–5 require each used-up species to have some internal production route, giving a semi-organization. This route-existence condition can fail quantitatively: `a -> b`, `b -> a`, and decay of both species satisfy it although both disappear. See pp. 2–4 / PDF pp. 2–4.

Definition 6 strengthens the test: there must exist a flux vector `v` that is positive on every reaction supported by `C`, zero on unsupported reactions, and has nonnegative net production `(Nv)_i` for every species `i` in `C`. Definition 7 calls a closed, mass-maintaining set an organization. This is an existence test, not a measurement of the realized trajectory's flux. The article's 2007 terminology must not silently replace the preprint's distinction between qualitative self-maintenance and mass-maintenance. See pp. 4–5 / PDF pp. 4–5.

For dynamic observations, an abstraction `phi(x)` selects present species from a concrete state. Definition 13 uses concentration `x_i > Theta`; Theorem 1 says a fixed point of the specified ODE is an instance of an organization under its stated positivity and threshold conditions. The converse is not asserted: an organization need not possess a fixed point, and a feasible flux need not be realized by the kinetics. The text distinguishes discrete extinction from ODE concentrations tending toward zero, and discusses thresholding. See pp. 9–13 / PDF pp. 9–13.

**Observer choices and limitations.** Species identity, reaction identity/stoichiometry, the reaction universe including inflows/outflows, spatial aggregation, presence threshold, and the mapping from a VM event to a “reaction” are all analyst/model choices. Without them COT cannot extract a body from bytes. A species organization can have multiple spatial instances or mix disconnected sites; set membership alone does not locate their boundaries. Logs of realized reactions may suggest a network, but absence from a finite log cannot establish that a reaction is impossible. Boolean closure and feasibility are binary. Flux magnitudes, abundance margins, or threshold sweeps could supply graded diagnostics, but such extensions are proposals here, not a graded-individuality algorithm established in the read version.

### BFF: finite copying robustness of a supplied tape

**Source findings.** The detector begins with a given 64-byte program and tests copy descendants against noise partners. Section 2 and Fig. 1 describe nine independent paths, each with five executions; on each generation the previous second half becomes the first and noise fills the second. At the end, the first-half score counts positions matching the original program in at least three paths; the second-half score counts positions sharing a value in at least three paths without requiring that value to match the original. Taking the smaller count accommodates programs that alternate byte values. The score is an integer from 0 to 64; classification uses at least 48, an acknowledged arbitrary threshold. See pp. 2–4, Algorithm 1 and Fig. 1.

The method misses copies shifted to different positions and programs requiring ecological partners excluded by the noise assay. It can accept a long-lived finite copying chain whose essential copying mechanism is eventually destroyed; the authors give a periodic-background counterexample in footnote 2 on p. 3. Five generations and noise partners test finite robustness, not indefinite viability or arbitrary collective reproduction. Tape length, cut point, first-position execution, noise distribution, generation count, branch count, positional equality, and threshold are prior choices. It is not a general individuality or body-boundary measure.

**Source/code discrepancy, not a reproduced result.** Algorithm 1 prints `i in [0..9]`, whereas the prose/Fig. 1 specify nine paths; the paper is internally inconsistent if that range is inclusive. The paper links the official [CUBFF `common_language.h`](https://github.com/paradigms-of-intelligence/cubff/blob/main/common_language.h). The [raw `main` file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/paradigms-of-intelligence/cubff/refs/heads/main/common_language.h) inspected on 2026-11-15 instead has `CheckSelfRep`, `kNumIters = 13`, four extra generations after the first, and agreement `count > kNumIters / 4` (thus at least four). It reuses a branch's noise across generations. This mutable code is supporting evidence for version sensitivity, not a pinned implementation of v1; no code was executed. The attempted commit-history API lookup failed. Any future reproduction must choose and archive the exact paper/code interpretation.

## C2: Observables, cost, and causal limits

Let `m` be the number of analyst-selected primitive variables or species, `N` the number of sampled transitions, `a` the number of candidate system states, `b` the number of environmental states, `r` the reaction count, `h` the maximum rule support size, and `L` a program's byte length. Bounds in this section are **our asymptotic estimates**, unless marked source statements. They are not benchmark results.

| Approach | Required observations and replay availability | Computational cost and limitations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ITI | A consistently encoded series of candidate and environmental states at chosen lag(s), plus a specified ensemble/window. Replay can supply these if snapshots or reconstructible deltas contain the variables. A single deterministic seed is not itself a probability distribution or a guarantee of sufficient samples. | Once states are encoded, sparse counting is `O(N)` per candidate and the triple table has at most `min(N,a^2 b)` occupied entries. Raw state spaces grow exponentially with the number of variables; sparse counts do not remove estimation bias or sample scarcity. Searching all oriented nontrivial system/environment subsets has `2^m-2` candidates; arbitrary full partitions number the Bell number `B_m`. Candidate restrictions reduce cost by imposing prior structure. The source provides no optimal search or finite-sample complexity guarantee (p. 221). |
| AlChemy | Canonical object identities, every tested ordered pair, successful/elastic outcome, reduction/filter limits, abundances and dilution events. Ordinary trajectory logs show realized production and occupancy; unobserved pair products require an explicit assay or derivation. | For a bounded candidate set, exhaustive pair testing uses `O(m^2)` reductions, each with a declared work limit. Repeating closure may expand the set without terminating. Exhaustive candidate center/subset search can involve `2^m` subsets; grammar discovery is an additional unresolved task. Source statement: its standard protocol caps reduction at 10,000 steps, intermediate size at 4,000 characters and normal-form size at 1,000 (§5.3); these are model budgets, not an asymptotic complexity theorem. |
| COT | Stable species/reaction identity, stoichiometry, inflow/outflow rules, concrete counts/concentrations and state-to-species abstraction. Complete configured rules may permit static analysis; finite event logs provide only an observed subnetwork and realized fluxes. | One candidate closure check scans `O(rh)` support entries; naive closure expansion needs at most `m` species-adding rounds for a finite universe. A naive all-subsets search has `2^m` candidates plus a linear-feasibility problem per surviving set. Rational linear programming is polynomial in matrix dimensions and encoded bit length for each fixed candidate; enumeration remains exponential. Source statement: finding all organizations appears difficult, and semi-organization checks can precede mass-maintenance checks (p. 5); no timing bound for TIDE is supplied. |
| BFF | Candidate tape bytes, interpreter/dialect, execution budget, cut point, noise generation and assay results. Replay can extract a candidate; the detector then performs new controlled executions. A stored natural soup trajectory usually lacks the required noise trials. | With `B` branches, `G` generations and evaluation limit `T`, evaluation is `O(BGT)`. Paper parameters suggest 45 evaluations per candidate, subject to the branch-count inconsistency; inspected mutable code uses 65. Source says parameters trade speed against false positives; it does not give a runtime theorem. |

**Observational versus interventional causality.** ITI's stochastic channels and graphical model are mechanistic assumptions; MI estimated from passive trajectories is predictive dependence under the chosen representation. It does not by itself identify the effect of changing an environmental channel, removing a component, or constructing a boundary. AlChemy/COT rule-based closure evaluates possible transformations within a specified model; it differs from empirical frequency and from a proof that a realized population survives. BFF's noise-partner copy chains are already a controlled assay of one copying capability. The AlChemy Level 2 inoculation proposal and component-removal repair tests require interventions. Replay makes the source history reproducible; it supplies counterfactual evidence only if the corresponding intervention is actually run under an explicit manifest. No such interventions were run here.

**Graded boundaries.** ITI scores are naturally continuous for each hard candidate split; this is not fractional site membership. AlChemy combines invariant membership with a fluctuating maintained periphery. COT membership/feasibility is binary under a selected abstraction, and BFF's integer score is graded before thresholding but assumes tape boundaries. None establishes one scalar that jointly measures all six proposed permeability channels. A hard partition can support graded diagnostics without making every membership decision observer-independent.

## TIDE proposals, separated from source findings

These are candidate analysis designs, not instructions to implement a kernel contract.

1. **Retain primitive identities and infer group extent.** Compare candidate sets of sites/threads or spatiotemporal supports without assigning ecological roles. Start with explicit locality/size bounds and report them; include tests where a candidate crosses ownership boundaries so ownership does not predetermine the answer. Track the same candidate in fixed and moving coordinates when relevant. Whether a “body” may overlap another, split, merge, or contain inactive residual bytes remains a question.
2. **Report a vector of channel observations before a single individuality score.** For each candidate and window, distinguish crossing read/fetch/write/damage/material/signal events, their opportunities or attempts, their allowed/denied/failed outcomes, and any actual resource debits. These channels need not all exist in R0. An absence of successful crossing can mean no demand, no opportunity, failed addressing, or protection; it is not by itself a permeability estimate. Separate information flows from conserved-material transfers and from VM actions.
3. **Use complementary diagnostics.** Integer event/count summaries can characterize realized exchange, turnover and construction/repair costs. ITI lenses can test temporal integrity under declared coarse-grainings. An AlChemy/COT-style analysis is meaningful only after explicitly defining VM object/species/reaction identity and scope; a provisional observed event network must be labeled incomplete. A BFF-style assay should remain a replication check for a defined extracted program/group, with partner and displacement cases, rather than silently defining all individuals as fixed copyable tapes.
4. **Preserve ambiguity and sensitivity.** Keep raw scores, denominators, sampling support and the candidate alternatives. Sweep lag, observation window, candidate size penalty and presence/score thresholds. Report multiple supported extents rather than forcing one border when evidence is nested or weak. Any weighted sum across channels would introduce observer-selected weights and units and needs its own justification.
5. **Reserve causal claims for later controlled comparisons.** If authorized after R0 is settled, separately test component removal, channel restriction, and reconstruction/repair while holding unrelated mechanisms fixed. A survival or copying difference under one intervention is evidence for that context, not universal membership. This pass proposes no experiment configuration and executes none.

## Open R0 logging questions

The following are information needs for discussion, **not an approved schema, persistence change, or required field list**.

- Which atomic event identities, phase/snapshot references and deterministic ordering would let a replay distinguish intended operations from resolved effects? Are read and fetch source bytes recoverable, including failed operations and simultaneous conflicting writes?
- How would one reconstruct thread/site/allocation identity through creation, death, reuse, splitting or grouping without treating ownership IDs as body labels? What time resolution preserves both short crossings and slower repair?
- For any implemented channel, what defines a crossing opportunity, attempted crossing and successful effect? Are denial, invalid addressing, no demand and conflict loss distinguishable? Can damaged or overwritten bytes be attributed to the actual resolved event rather than a later inferred genotype difference?
- If construction or maintenance has a physical cost, what are its source, sink and actual debit on success/failure? Can component production/reclamation and transport be counted separately from copying informational bytes? No material or signal semantics are assumed merely because those candidate channels were named.
- Is the full relevant transition/reaction universe reconstructible from a kernel revision and configuration, or can an analyst certify only the sampled event network? How are analysis equivalence classes and changing species definitions versioned independently of the kernel?
- Which exact initial state, configuration, random-stream identity and kernel revision can be archived for an offline intervention? Should floating-point analysis outputs have their own versioned estimator/precision receipt while replay validation continues to use the kernel state hash?
