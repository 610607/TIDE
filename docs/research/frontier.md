# Current Frontier: A Bounded First Pass

Searched: 2026-11-15. Scope: research positioning for TIDE's supplied-ancestor, local address-based VM; no kernel rules are settled by this note.

## Finding

The strongest near-term question is how local access to other programs changes the evolution of replication strategies. Spatial Stringmol is the closest selected mechanistic precedent. Recent BFF work makes a crucial measurement distinction: first appearance, population spread, and sustained adaptive innovation are separate outcomes. ASAL and continuous substrates offer useful comparisons, but externally searched visual novelty is a different claim from endogenous evolution.

This is a five-work shortlist, not an exhaustive survey or a novelty assessment. Cached 2024 BFF is an additional contextual baseline, already in the core review. No simulations or source-code reproduction were performed.

## Included works

### F1. Spatial Stringmol: replication strategy evolves

Simon J. Hickinbotham, Susan Stepney, Paulien Hogeweg. 2021. *Nothing in evolution makes sense except in the light of parasitism: evolution of complex replication strategies*. Published: *Royal Society Open Science* 8:210441.

[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.210441) | [Cached PDF](../references/pdf/hickinbotham-et-al-2021-spatial-stringmol.pdf) | [Institutional PDF](https://research-portal.uu.nl/ws/files/110685157/rsos.210441.pdf).

- **Read:** Results §§2.1-2.3, Discussion §§3.2-3.3, Conclusion §4, Methods §5; the cached PDF includes a repository cover page.
- **Source finding:** Twelve of 20 runs went extinct; eight continued to two million timesteps. In survivors, replication mechanisms acquired recognition, self-scanning, and counter-parasite behavior; expensive defenses could subsequently disappear. Spatial organization helped preserve replication against exploitation.
- **Limit:** This is a bound-pair automata chemistry with different CPU and memory ownership from TIDE. Finite surviving trajectories establish neither indefinite innovation nor a general guarantee that parasitism increases complexity.
- **TIDE inference:** Trace and test replication pathways, including costs and dependencies, rather than measuring genome length alone. Include extinction and loss of defenses in the result, not only successful episodes.

### F2. BFF follow-up: detecting origins separately from takeover

Charlotte Knierim, Luca Versari, Robert Obryk, Blaise Agüera y Arcas, Rif A. Saurous. 2026. *BFF: Simple explanations for complex phenomena*. Reviewed version: arXiv:2607.01483v1, submitted 1 July 2026; preprint, no journal publication verified.

[Version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01483v1) | [Cached PDF](../references/pdf/knierim-et-al-2026-bff-explanations-v1.pdf) | [arXiv DOI](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.01483) | [Detector §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01483v1#S2) | [Comparisons §§3.1-3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01483v1#S3.SS1) | [Blocking §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01483v1#S3.SS3).

- **Read:** Full-text §§1-3.3, including Algorithm 1 and Table 1.
- **Source finding:** Distributionally tuned random sampling/mutation can find detected replicators at least as efficiently as dyadic interaction under the tested comparisons. Blocking merger ancestry depth/width does not eliminate first appearance, although it inhibits takeover.
- **Limit:** Mainly short, fixed-length BFF tapes, especially `bff_selfmove`; the detector uses researcher-chosen finite-chain/byte-match thresholds, misses shifted copies, and has a documented eventual-failure example. This is not a result about every substrate or sustained innovation.
- **TIDE inference:** Use direct replay alongside population statistics, and distinguish discovery, establishment, spread, and subsequent strategy change.

### F3. ASAL: an external search objective

Akarsh Kumar, Chris Lu, Louis Kirsch, Yujin Tang, Kenneth O. Stanley, Phillip Isola, David Ha. *Automating the Search for Artificial Life with Foundation Models*. First preprint 2024; reviewed arXiv:2412.17799v2, 16 May 2025. A 2025 article is indexed in *Artificial Life* 31(3):368-396, DOI 10.1162/ARTL.a.8; publisher text was inaccessible here, so findings below refer to v2.

[Version/full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17799v2) | [Cached PDF](../references/pdf/kumar-et-al-2025-asal-v2.pdf) | [Author project](https://asal.sakana.ai/) | [Article DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/ARTL.a.8) | [Publication metadata](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40911297/).

- **Read:** Methods §§3.1-3.3, Experiments §§4.1-4.5, Discussion §5.
- **Source finding:** Foundation-model embeddings guide searches for prompt matching, historical visual novelty, and diverse simulations. The open-endedness experiment searches Life-like CA; it does not demonstrate the same objective on Lenia, Boids, or NCA.
- **Limit:** The evaluator, representation, optimization, and sometimes renderer are supplied externally. Human-aligned image novelty does not by itself test heredity, adaptive strategy innovation, or reproduction-driven selection.
- **TIDE inference:** Such search may later explore configurations or aid observation. Keep its objective outside the kernel and distinguish searched discoveries from autonomous evolutionary outcomes.

### F4. Flow-Lenia: resource and measurement choices matter

Erwan Plantec, Gautier Hamon, Mayalen Etcheverry, Bert Wang-Chak Chan, Pierre-Yves Oudeyer, Clément Moulin-Frier. 2025. *Flow-Lenia: Emergent evolutionary dynamics in mass conservative continuous cellular automata*. Published: *Artificial Life* 31(2):228-248. Reviewed author manuscript: arXiv:2506.08569v1, 10 June 2025.

[Version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08569v1) | [Cached PDF](../references/pdf/plantec-et-al-2025-flow-lenia-evolution-v1.pdf) | [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08569v1) | [Article DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00471).

- **Read:** Parameter embedding §3.1; directed versus intrinsic experiments §§4.2-4.3; intrinsic results §5.3; Discussion §6.
- **Source finding:** Localized parameters allow competing update rules in one continuous world. Directed behavior optimization and intrinsic evolution are separate experiments. Intrinsic runs use mutation beams and five seeds per condition; mass normalization reverses the raw evolutionary-activity ordering of environmental variants.
- **Limit:** A distinct parameter vector counts as a species regardless of phenotype. The authors acknowledge that this can misrepresent evolutionary units. Mass conservation and parameter transport are substrate choices, not transferable proof of an OEE recipe.
- **TIDE inference:** Report abundance/resource normalization and functional changes separately from genotype diversity. Do not infer that adding food or dissipation necessarily improves innovation.

### F5. Flow-Lenia 2026 extension: search and scale remain separate questions

Thomas Michel, Marko Cvjetko, Gautier Hamon, Pierre-Yves Oudeyer, Clément Moulin-Frier. *Exploring Flow-Lenia Universes with a Curiosity-driven AI Scientist: Discovering Diverse Ecosystem Dynamics*. Reviewed arXiv:2505.15998v4, 4 June 2026: extended version of an ALife 2025 paper, proceedings pp. 633-643. The 2026 extension's separate publication status was not verified.

[Version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15998v4) | [Cached PDF](../references/pdf/michel-et-al-2026-flow-lenia-universes-v4.pdf) | [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15998v4) | [Conference DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/ISAL.a.896) | [Author project](https://developmentalsystems.org/Exploring-Flow-Lenia-Universes/).

- **Read:** Methods §§3.2-3.4, mixing-rule comparison §4.1, scaling §4.4, Discussion §§5-5.1.
- **Source finding:** Changing parameter-mixing rules changes evolutionary activity. External IMGEP search covers more chosen metric space than random search. Scaling selected configurations reveals larger structures, while normalizing activity/compression by mass, cells, and time reverses some raw growth trends.
- **Limit:** The authors leave macro-pattern persistence unresolved and describe much ecological interpretation as qualitative. Broader search coverage and activity are not equivalent to sustained endogenous adaptive innovation.
- **TIDE inference:** A promising small run needs longer, larger, independently evaluated follow-up. Treat metric sensitivity and transient behavior as research questions.

## What the BFF update does and does not change

The cached baseline is Blaise Agüera y Arcas, Jyrki Alakuijala, James Evans, Ben Laurie, Alexander Mordvintsev, Eyvind Niklasson, Ettore Randazzo, Luca Versari (2024), *Computational Life: How Well-formed, Self-replicating Programs Emerge from Simple Interaction*, arXiv:2406.19108v2, 2 August 2024. [Cached PDF](../references/pdf/aguera-y-arcas-et-al-2024-computational-life-v2.pdf) | [Version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19108v2).

The original explicitly allows random-initialization or mutation origins (Introduction, printed p. 3). Its compression-based proxy measures domination, and it acknowledges destruction before takeover (§2.1, printed pp. 5-6). Its Discussion calls post-origin evidence of greater complexity anecdotal (printed p. 18). Therefore, do not turn it into a universal assertion that interaction is necessary for origins. F2 supplies a stronger direct-detection comparison; it does not undo observed no-background-mutation emergence. The cached v2 contains no merger-depth/width experiment; F2's blocking setup should not be retroactively attributed to an experiment read in that version.

## Candidate research questions: proposals only

1. **Access and evolved replication:** Starting from a supplied ancestor, how does local access to neighboring data and executable code affect the appearance and persistence of functionally distinct replication strategies? Closest selected work: F1, with Tierra as an existing core precedent; F2 motivates distinct detection endpoints. Candidate controls: self-only, neighbor-read, and neighbor-read-plus-fetch access, with own-allocation writes and other rules held fixed. Establish expressivity without mutation first; then use lineage sampling and counterfactual replay to test which external instruction/data sources are necessary for reproduction. This is a testable TIDE question, not a verified novelty claim.
2. **Dead code as an ecological resource:** Does retaining accessible unowned code after death change the persistence or repertoire of replication strategies relative to clearing it? Closest selected concepts: BFF's byte provenance and F1's evolving dependencies; neither directly answers this question. This is more weakly grounded and requires a targeted reclamation/Tierra-successor search. Keep reclamation intervals and occupation effects explicit; distinguish causal use of dead code from mere nearby bytes.
3. **Robustness to update semantics:** Do strategy changes and dependency relationships found under synchronous execution persist under alternative deterministic conflict policies and the sequential random-order comparison? Closest selected work: F1's spatial mechanism and the 2024 BFF spatial experiments; neither is a matched TIDE scheduler comparison. Use fixed populations and mutation-free replay before broader evolution runs. This is a robustness study; its scientific novelty remains unassessed.

For each question, prespecify finite observation windows and report survival, extinction, stagnation, establishment, and loss of strategies. A new byte string, higher activity, longer program, or more novel image is insufficient alone to establish adaptive innovation. Dependence and benefit should be established by controlled replay; thresholds and functional equivalence still need agreement.
