# Birth orientation and passive transport in six-byte fields

Baseline 7392f81. Replay every seed 19521–21568 to tick 2048 with the archived six-byte configuration. Keep the original pilot and replication blocks of 1,024 seeds separate and report pooled results. Use the same seed in four arms. The founder faces east in all arms. The stationary east arm is the original world. The stationary random-birth arm sets each newborn's initial bearing independently and uniformly among the four directions. A parent's normal reset after division still faces east. The two transport arms retain eastward births and differ from baseline only in the environmental exchange step.

After each synchronous instruction commit, draw one of the four disjoint nearest-neighbor perfect matchings of the even torus (horizontal or vertical, even or odd parity). In the low and high arms, select each matched edge with probability 1/64 or 1/4 per tick, using the same deterministic draw for both thresholds. Exchange both entire banks if at least one is occupied, neither is reserved and neither owns a reservation. A failed selection or ineligible edge has no effect. Free residual bytes are exchanged without duplication. A moving individual's sequence, stable identity, age, CPU state, direction and local pointer offsets travel with it. Other individuals keep their pointers to the same lattice addresses. Read permissions are uniform and external instruction fetch is off. Newborns may move at their birth commit and first execute next tick. Decisions use domain-separated seed/tick/site counters independent of the VM substitution draw. This study defines experimental rules for these fields.

Count a potential encounter once per ordered pair of individuals in a world. At an instruction's opening, they must be cardinal neighbors, both alive, have different sequences, and the potential reader must carry an S instruction. This inclusive screen includes programs that never execute S. Also count pairs ever seen in the current bearing, and those ever seen in that bearing with either an owned allocation or an available neighbor. Count actually read pairs and pairs that yield an intact birth separately. An exact external birth requires all six aligned bytes from one other living individual, its sequence different from the executor's, without substitution. Trace identities across moves. Report its direction at first read. Multiple births from a pair count separately in the birth total and once in the contact conversion fraction.

For an exact external newborn with complete age follow-up by the cutoff, score further transmission if it executes an exact self-copy or supplies an intact living template for another exact birth. Score the union and both routes; count each enrolled individual once. Record incomplete follow-up separately. Compare newborn orientation via the east share and the total exact births. Compare transport via unlike-sequence donor encounters, conversion to exact birth per encountered pair, and subsequent transmission. Report total births, exposure in living individual-ticks, absolute exact births, and per-seed outcomes including extinction and stagnation. Use matched seed differences for counts. The mutation schedule remains site-dependent; the estimates describe the full change in these field dynamics.

Before interpreting results, verify fixed mutation-free examples of identity-preserving exchange, pointer locality, reservation exclusion and conservation. Compare the stationary east arm against the archived population ledger, state hash and source-direction census. Repeat all four arms in reverse instruction and matching traversal with a separately optimized build. Archive all seed rows and the provenance of exact unlike-sequence births.
