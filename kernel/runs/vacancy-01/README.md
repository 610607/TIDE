# A naturally evolved reader copied a living neighbor into two daughters

In seed 2437, two adjacent digital individuals acquired the same five-byte reader program through separate copy substitutions. The western individual won space through ordinary competition and copied all five bytes from its living eastern neighbor twice. Both daughters reproduced by reading neighboring banks. Their four offspring carried other programs. This is one natural family with transmission through one generation in 2,560 prospectively sampled worlds. In a mutation-free assay the mother's own donor background supported three successive reader generations. ## What happened in seed 2437

At ticks 1663 and 1665, two different parents running the independent ancestor sequence `R.CLD` each copied its own bytes with the same `.` to `S` substitution. The two resulting `RSCLD` newborns occupied sites 20 and 21. At tick 1666 the eastern reader was alive and the western reader had no free neighbor. At tick 1676 a daughter site opened to the north and the western reader won it. It produced a daughter at tick 1688 and another at tick 1741. Each birth used five unchanged bytes from site 21, occupant birth tick 1665. Both birth ticks fall inside the original field census. The first daughter read `R.CLD` from its own reservation and a substitution produced `R.CJD`. It later copied that living child's bytes into two more offspring. The second daughter copied `R.CLD` from a living neighbor. At the 512-tick follow-up, two descendants remained in the maternal family and the uninterrupted reader lineage had ended. These permission forks were selected after observing the positive field case. They attribute births within that family. In the existing three-generation assay, the mother's observed live donor supported five external reads and one intact reader birth in each generation. With focal access denied, the arena reached an exact cycle at tick 4. The independent ancestor reproduced in all three generations with either permission. The first daughter's field source was a reservation and fell outside the paired live-donor assay. On the second daughter's actual live background, only the first assay generation used an external read. None of the four field grandchild events met the earlier mother-and-daughter serial confirmation criterion. The tested permission controls access to data in the neighbor's bank. ## Census and paired interventions

All fields used the reference VM at `1b4debb`, a 16 × 4 torus, one independent site-36 ancestor, external reads enabled, external fetch disabled, maximum age 80, retained bytes, first-free placement and 20,000 copy substitutions per million successful writes. Each field ran 2,048 ticks. Each eligible encounter fork ran a further 512 ticks from its opening. We counted the first opportunity per reader to execute reserve while an exact reader occupied its eastern site, before any attempted copy. The primary endpoint was a first focal birth of an exact reader, with all five unchanged bytes traced to that particular live donor. The screen targets this sequence and opportunity window. **Vacancy alone.** The local intervention retired one non-eastern neighbor holding no allocation, preserved its bytes and let all CPUs compete for the new site. All five preferred releases in the first experiment lost the opening contest. In the historical directional sensitivity test, two of eight local alternatives met the endpoint. Both were in seed 561. **Winning the opening.** The next experiment changed only which CPU won the first claim after the same release. Subsequent steps used ordinary arbitration. In replication, granting the first claim added successes in seeds 2374 and 2526. For the two preferred local releases already won under ordinary arbitration, seeds 1254 and 2019, the priority branch had the same final state hash. **Time available.** After an immediate reservation on tick `t`, a reader attempts its five copies on ticks `t+2` through `t+10` at two-tick intervals and divides on `t+12`. A donor can supply bytes on its death tick because reads use the opening snapshot. The focal must survive through division. All seven preferred forks meeting both age conditions produced intact first daughters after an immediate reservation. The three historical cases and four new replication cases account for these seven. In four other cases the donor died too early and in one the focal died on the planned division tick. No first product in the granted branches had a copy substitution. The counts describe these 12 encounters and the specified mutation draws. [Age windows and observed births](priority/timing.tsv)

**Later generations.** Among the 109 priority-study branches, two historical alternatives produced two consecutive intact transmissions entirely from live readers. Both involved a forced opening to the west, in seeds 419 and 569. The daughter landed west of its mother and later read all five bytes from that still-living mother to make a reader granddaughter. In seed 1531 an intervened branch reached three transmissions when reserved and free bytes also counted. No uninterrupted reader lineage was alive at the follow-up endpoint in any branch. The alternate directions share founders and do not add independent seeds. The untreated seed 2437 also shows why access alone is insufficient for a longer chain. Both daughters used that access and copied sequences that placed other instructions in their offspring. [Births and source identity at every link](priority/products.tsv)

## Reproduction

Run from the repository root with C++20, Python 3.10 or newer, GNU Make and `patch`. The scanner and the natural permission audit use the reference VM directly. The vacancy and one-tick arbitration adapters are applied to build-directory copies. Each ordinary follow-up checks its final state hash against the separate reference scan. Both the reference and intervention test suites passed. The 22 output tables below the study directory reproduced byte-for-byte in an `-O3` build with reversed field traversal. The small serial arenas use their original traversal in both builds. [Source and data checksums](revision.sha256)

```sh
make -C kernel all test
make -C kernel/runs/vacancy-01 all test
study=kernel/runs/vacancy-01
bin=kernel/build/vacancy
out=kernel/build/vacancy/results
mkdir -p "$out/priority"
"$bin/scan" "$out"
"$bin/fork" "$out"
"$bin/scan" "$out/priority" --additional
"$bin/fork" "$out/priority" --priority --include "$out/encounters.tsv"
python3 -B "$study/analyze.py" "$out" "$out/priority" "$bin"
for table in "$study"/*.tsv; do cmp "$table" "$out/${table##*/}" || exit 1; done
for table in "$study"/priority/*.tsv; do cmp "$table" "$out/priority/${table##*/}" || exit 1; done
shasum -a 256 -c "$study/priority-frozen.sha256"
shasum -a 256 -c "$study/revision.sha256"
```

For the second build, pass `BUILD=../../build/vacancy-replay 'CXXFLAGS=-O3 -Wall -Wextra -Werror'` to study Make, use that binary directory and a separate output directory, and append `--reverse` to the two scans, both forks and the analysis command.
