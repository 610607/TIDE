# Can a changed replication trait survive its first observed carrier?

Freeze against 9609fa5 before running new seeds. Use the stationary six-origin field with one independent R..CLD founder at site 36, a 16 × 4 torus, first-free placement, synchronous updates, age limit 80, retained memory, neighbor data reads, local instruction fetch and 20,000 ppm copy substitution. The trait under test is reproductive dependence on external reads in the individual's own birth environment. Use two consecutive, fixed blocks of 512 new seeds (25665–26176 and 26177–26688). Follow every field for 8,192 ticks. Use another 81 ticks solely to finish the lifetime assays of individuals born by the cutoff. Record all fields, including zero detections, stagnation and extinction. Use historical seed 19995 as a separate short-horizon positive control.

Record the CPU executor and the individual supplying six aligned living-source bytes at every birth. The supplier may be the executor itself or a different individual. Keep the six input and output bytes even when substitution occurs. Mixed and residual sources can establish an executor edge only. For every individual born within enrollment that produced any offspring with externally read bytes over its full lifetime, fork its natural birth state. Confirm dependence when ordinary reproduction succeeds, a focal-only external-read denial encounters a blocked external copy and produces zero offspring through death. Keep the same mutation draws and environment rules. Match each ordinary replay birth to the untreated history. Detect candidates by observed external-material offspring, without a genome filter.

Join consecutive confirmed individuals by direct executor or aligned living-template edges. Deduplicate a birth with the same executor and supplier. A root is a confirmed individual with no incoming edge from another confirmed individual. Primary endpoint: a world has an uninterrupted confirmed path containing a change in program between neighbors on the path, with a confirmed descendant born strictly after its root's committed death. A change along an executor edge can come from already different input bytes. Record copy substitutions separately. Report world counts per seed block, depth in individuals, elapsed ticks between first and last carrier births, number of distinct programs on a path, and world incidence of at least three distinct programs on a successful path. This latter endpoint diagnoses successive changes and does not test fitness improvement. Record the primary endpoint with roots born by tick 8030 separately. Describe observed terminal successors, including nonreproducers, reproducers without external-material offspring, candidates reproducing under denial, and births beyond enrollment.

Also trace material inheritance from confirmed carriers through any number of aligned single-living-donor births, allowing passive intermediates and substitutions. Score later confirmed carriers across these paths separately. Identify returns of a previously confirmed program when its live population is zero at the birth opening, a substitution regenerates it from a different input, and the new carrier is confirmed. Check whether any earlier confirmed carrier of those bytes is an ancestor through executor or aligned living-template edges, including intermediate individuals of all kinds. Report returns outside those recorded pedigrees separately. Partial or residual-byte ancestry can escape that test. Matching bytes alone never extends a path.

Archive field ledgers, all tested individuals, the relevant executor and material descendants, origins, traces and world endpoints. Compare the two blocks directly. Validate the fixed-program controls, natural and ordinary replay births, checkpoint hashes and separately optimized reverse traversal. Interpret a negative or short-chain result as a bounded property of this configuration. No new transition is introduced.
