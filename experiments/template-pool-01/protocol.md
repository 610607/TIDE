# Does one chain change what a pool of simple pieces builds?

Freeze this exploratory digital assay against TIDE revision 6554cfe before examining outcomes. Run seeds 27713–28736 and 28737–29760 as two fixed blocks. A chamber contains 64 free pieces of each of two types, four independent growing ends, and a budget of 160 encounters per round. One chamber is well mixed. The symbols, probabilities and length are model choices. This assay measures the consequences of a supplied local pairing rule.

At each encounter independently propose an end, a piece type with equal probability, a guide choice and an integer acceptance draw in [0,7]. An empty end without a guide docks a uniformly selected free completed chain if one exists. Each guide can occupy one end at a time and stays there until completion. A bare end accepts on 0 only. With a guide, the position is the current length of the end; opposite symbols at this position accept on 0 through 6 and equal symbols on 0 only. A position-blind guide instead accepts either type on 0 through 3. A missing piece or a rejected encounter leaves the growing chain and free stock unchanged. Docking persists even when attachment fails. Each success moves exactly one free piece to the end. At length six, release the old guide and the intact product. Every new product can itself guide immediately by the same position rule. No sequence receives a special role or fitness score. The starting chain contributes six externally supplied bound pieces and is never a product.

Round one has four arms: no added chain, one active chain 001011, one active composition-matched reorder 011100, and one position-blind 001011. Use common counter-indexed proposals in each seed. Report all completed products by sequence, partial chains, failures and remaining stock. Primary round-one contrast: the paired change in the share of all products equal to the bitwise complement of 001011 between active 001011 and empty. Check the same endpoint against position-blind 001011. Test specificity by asking whether each active seed sequence preferentially enriches its own complement relative to the reordered seed.

At the fixed boundary select one first-round product uniformly among all completions, including imperfect and unguided ones. Use a separate random draw. Remove everything else, including the founder, and move the selected actual chain to a fresh chamber with the same stock and encounter budget. Run matched branches with the transferred chain active or position-blind; all its new products remain ordinarily active. When there is no completion, transfer nothing and run both branches. Primary round-two contrast within the 001011 arm: difference in share complementary to the transferred product across the two branches. Separately count worlds where the founder directly guided the selected exact complement, and that very product directly guided at least one exact return of the founder sequence in the new chamber. Report founder-sequence abundance irrespective of ancestry as well. This transfer tests one further round under renewed supply.

Use worlds as paired units. Empty output or a missing transfer contributes zero to the corresponding world share, with counts reported explicitly. Publish pooled product spectra as well as per-world contrasts and both seed blocks. Archive the actual guide identity for each completed product, each selected identity, and the balance of both piece types. Verify replay with different optimization levels and a reversed chamber-end audit order, plus deterministic small controls. Keep all settings and outcomes separate from the reference VM.
